Warranty help

How to get your warranty to cover the repair

Most covered repairs cost the customer nothing, and manufacturers approve more out-of-warranty repairs than people expect when they are asked the right way. This is the playbook, step by step. It is general information and policies vary by brand and plan.

Before you call

Get these together first

A complete file gets approved faster and avoids the call-back loop.

  • Brand, full model number and serial number from the data plate.
  • Purchase date and the receipt or proof of purchase, plus the product registration confirmation if you have one.
  • The exact symptom, when it started, and any error code on the display.
  • A short video or photos of the fault and the data plate.
  • Invoices from any earlier repairs on the same unit.
  • The warranty terms. Coverage often differs by component; sealed systems and major parts are frequently covered longer than the basic warranty.

In warranty

How the claim works

  1. Call the number in your owner's manual or use the manufacturer's online claim page. If you have a home warranty or service plan, call the plan administrator instead.
  2. Give the model, serial number, purchase date, symptom and error code.
  3. Ask for a case number and the name of the person you spoke with. Ask them to confirm by email.
  4. Ask which servicer they will dispatch, and whether they can assign Tradewerx in your area. We are working on manufacturer and administrator network listings, so send us the claim or dispatch number and provider name and we will take it from there where we can.
  5. Keep every email and case number in one place.

Out of warranty

How to ask for a goodwill repair

A goodwill repair is a free or discounted repair a manufacturer approves even though no warranty requires it. It is discretionary, so tone and preparation matter.

  1. Ask for it by name. Say "goodwill," "policy adjustment" or "customer relations coverage."
  2. Lead with facts. Purchase date, price paid, how recently the warranty ended, repeat failures of the same part, and any earlier service.
  3. Mention loyalty. Other appliances of the same brand in your home matter to them.
  4. Ask about known issues. "Is there a service bulletin, recall or extended coverage program for this failure on this model?" Also check the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recall list at cpsc.gov.
  5. Ask for part of it. If a full repair is refused, ask for parts only or a split of labor.
  6. Escalate calmly. Front-line agent, then supervisor, then customer relations or executive resolution. Get a case number and name each time.
  7. Put it in writing. Email a short summary after every call.
  8. Ask the seller. A dealer that sold a premium unit can sometimes get the manufacturer to reconsider.

Phone script: "My [brand model] was purchased on [date] for [price] and the [component] has failed [X] months after the warranty ended. I would like to request a goodwill repair. Are there any service bulletins or extended coverage for this failure? I would like a case number and the name of the person I am speaking with."

Other places coverage hides

Check these before paying

  • Credit card extended warranty. Many cards add roughly a year of coverage on eligible purchases. You usually need the receipt and card statement and must file within a short window, often 60 to 90 days of the failure. Check your card's benefits guide.
  • Retailer or purchase-time protection plan. Check the paperwork from the day you bought the unit.
  • Home warranty or service plan. Open the claim with the administrator; they dispatch a technician from their network.
  • Builder or new-home warranty. Appliances in a new home are often covered for the first year or more.
  • Implied warranty. In North Carolina, goods sold by a merchant carry an implied warranty of merchantability, and the limitation period for sales contracts is generally four years from purchase. It is a legal claim rather than a repair program, and it will not cover normal wear. Talk to an attorney for advice on your situation.
  • Independent service does not void a warranty by itself. Under the federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act, a company generally cannot void your warranty only because you used someone else's part or service. Warranty repairs themselves go through the servicer the warranty names.

Common questions

Does using an independent technician void my warranty?

Not by itself. Under the federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act, a company generally cannot void your warranty only because you used a part or service from someone other than the manufacturer, unless it provides that part or service for free under the warranty. Covered repairs, however, are performed by the servicer the warranty designates, so start by opening the claim with the manufacturer or plan administrator.

How do I get a warranty claim or dispatch number?

Call the number in your owner's manual or use the manufacturer's online claim page. Give the model, serial number, purchase date and the exact symptom or error code, and ask for a case number and the servicer they will dispatch. Ask them to confirm by email.

What if my appliance is just out of warranty?

Ask the manufacturer for a goodwill repair or policy adjustment. It is discretionary, so be polite and persistent, escalate to customer relations, and lead with the purchase date, price, repeat failures and any service history.
